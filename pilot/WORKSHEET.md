# Business case worksheet and instructor key

All figures are fictional USD teaching assumptions. Costs are held fixed in sensitivity calculations. Results are undiscounted, steady-state estimates; ramp, seasonality, taxes and financing are excluded. This is capacity-equivalent value unless its conversion to avoided cost or useful output is evidenced.

| Input | Base case | Evidence needed in a real pilot |
|---|---:|---|
| Monthly tickets | 12,000 | Ticket-system export and volume trend |
| Baseline handling minutes | 12 | Representative observed baseline |
| Assisted fraction of all tickets | 50% | Product telemetry; eligibility and actual usage |
| Net minutes saved per assisted ticket | 3 | Includes review and correction, paired with quality |
| Fully loaded hourly labor rate | $30 | Finance-approved costing method |
| Realization factor | 70% | Documented redeployment or avoided-cost plan |
| Monthly software/model cost | $1,800 | Current quote and usage assumptions |
| Monthly integration support | $700 | Service ownership and effort estimate |
| Monthly evaluation/governance | $500 | Monitoring and control operating plan |
| Upfront implementation/training | $18,000 | Implementation scope and staffing |

Monthly released hours = tickets × assisted fraction × net saved minutes ÷ 60.

Recognized monthly value = released hours × hourly rate × realization factor.

Year-one cost = 12 × monthly recurring costs + upfront cost.

Year-one ROI = (12 × recognized monthly value − year-one cost) ÷ year-one cost.

Simple payback after full benefits begin = upfront cost ÷ (monthly recognized value − monthly recurring costs), only if the denominator is positive.

| Case | Assisted fraction | Monthly recognized value | Annual benefit | Year-one net | ROI |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Low coverage | 25% | $3,150 | $37,800 | −$16,200 | −30% |
| Base | 50% | $6,300 | $75,600 | $21,600 | 40% |
| High coverage | 75% | $9,450 | $113,400 | $59,400 | 110% |

Base payback = $18,000 ÷ $3,300 = 5.45 steady-state months. Break-even assisted fraction = $54,000 ÷ (12,000 × 3 ÷ 60 × $30 × 0.70 × 12) = 35.71%.

## Learner exercise

1. Set realization to 40% with base coverage. Recalculate first-year ROI and explain whether the proposal still works financially.
2. Explain why released capacity and cash savings require different evidence.
3. Identify two costs that may change with volume and propose a better cost model.
4. Write a pilot recommendation with an owner, value metric, quality guardrail and stop condition.

## Instructor key

At 40% realization, benefit is $3,600/month or $43,200/year, net benefit is −$10,800 and ROI is −20%. Released hours alone do not prove reduced payroll or increased revenue. Usage-related model costs and monitoring effort can vary; model fixed and variable components independently, avoiding double counting. A strong pilot recommendation specifies a pre-agreed decision rule, comparison design, accountable owner, quality evidence and a rollback path. Numerical quality thresholds must be justified by the business context rather than invented as universal standards.
