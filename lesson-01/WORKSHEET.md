# Lesson 01 — learner worksheet

Choose one task in your organization and complete these prompts:

1. What output does the business need? Be specific: a calculated amount, category, estimate, recommendation, draft or something else.
2. Can an explicit rule adequately specify the expected output? If not, what patterns or content would be needed?
3. Which approach appears suitable: ordinary automation, predictive ML, generative AI, or a combination? Explain the reasoning.
4. What baseline would you compare against?
5. What evidence would establish useful performance in the actual process?
6. What could go wrong, who would review the result, and who could stop the workflow?

## Example answer

Task: drafting retail product descriptions. Output: new text preserving approved product facts. Generative AI can assist drafting, with factual and style checks before publication. Baseline: the current drafting-and-review workflow. Evaluate unsupported claims, factual completeness, style suitability and net authoring time including correction. An accountable editor approves publication; a product-fact discrepancy blocks release. Avoid presenting faster text generation alone as proof of time savings.

## Review guidance

A good answer follows the task and its constraints rather than a preferred product name. Distinguish ordinary rule-based automation from the broader fact that some AI approaches also use rules. Recognize that a modern generative system is also an ML system. Give evidence suited to the output and business outcome.
