### Slide: Welcome: Why Learn AI Business Strategy?
<!-- id: m1-welcome -->
<!-- type: content-image -->
<!-- visual: image -->

Welcome to AWS Certified AI Business Strategist, AIB-C01. Think about the next time someone in a meeting proposes using AI. A convincing demonstration may show that a tool can do something impressive, but the organization still has to decide whether the idea deserves investment, what a useful result would look like, and who should own the outcome. This course helps you take part in that conversation with confidence. We will connect the certification to decisions you might face in a real role, and make the official AWS pages easy to navigate. In this opening module, you will decide whether the course fits your goals, understand the learning commitment, and see where the credential belongs in the wider certification landscape.

- Understand the opportunity
- Decide whether the course fits you
- Connect learning to a real business goal

### Slide: Why AWS Created a Business-Focused AI Credential
<!-- id: m1-why-business-judgment -->
<!-- type: content-image -->
<!-- visual: image -->

AWS announced this business-focused certification in September 2026 to recognize people who guide AI adoption alongside technical builders. Its launch article separates building a solution from deciding which opportunities to pursue, how to justify spending, and when a successful experiment should expand. You may already encounter that responsibility without having AI in your job title. Imagine a department with three promising proposals but funding for only one. Choosing well requires understanding the problem, the evidence and the constraints, then explaining a recommendation that other teams can act on. Our aim is to develop that reasoning. The certification can help communicate the capability, while the work you do with it is what makes the learning useful.

- Technical building and business decisions are different work
- AI responsibility can sit inside an existing role
- Practice recommendations you can explain and defend

### Slide: AI Adoption Is Not the Same as Business Value
<!-- id: m1-adoption-versus-value -->
<!-- type: content-image -->
<!-- visual: image -->

Here is a useful reason to look beyond the excitement around AI. In McKinsey's August 2026 survey, eighty percent of respondents reported improved individual productivity, while thirty-seven percent reported a positive contribution to their organization's earnings before interest and taxes. These are different measures, not a before-and-after experiment, and they do not prove that a particular product causes financial gains. They do illustrate a question worth asking: how does a benefit for one person become a useful organizational result? If an assistant saves an employee twenty minutes, what happens to that time? Does it reduce a backlog, improve service, or simply disappear into the working day? That is the kind of business reasoning we will practice.

- Individual productivity and enterprise value are different measures
- Ask how an improvement reaches a business outcome
- Read the date, sample and metric behind a statistic

### Slide: Who Is This Course For?
<!-- id: m1-audience -->
<!-- type: content-image -->

The intended audience includes product and program managers, business leaders, consultants, analysts, sales and business development professionals, and marketers. ### Slide: What Do You Need Before Starting?
<!-- id: m1-entry-expectations -->
<!-- type: content-image -->
<!-- visual: image -->

AWS recommends basic AI familiarity and around six months working with or alongside AI initiatives. Coding, hands-on AWS implementation and a previous AWS certification are not entry requirements. A recommendation for experience is different from a mandatory prerequisite. If you are new to the subject, bring an example you can revisit as the course progresses: perhaps a customer-support process, a document review task or an internal knowledge search. You do not need to claim experience you do not have. Your first goal is to build a vocabulary and learn how to examine a proposal. When a term is unfamiliar, pause, note it and return to it in the foundations section rather than feeling you must understand everything in the opening module.

- Basic AI familiarity is recommended
- Six months alongside AI initiatives is recommended
- No coding or prior AWS certification is required

### Slide: What This Course Develops—and What It Does Not
<!-- id: m1-scope-boundary -->
<!-- type: content-image -->
<!-- visual: image -->

A business strategist and an engineer can examine the same project at different levels. Suppose an organization wants to search its internal policies using an assistant. In this course, you might ask whether the information is suitable, how access should be controlled, how answers will be checked, and what result would justify a wider rollout. You will not be expected to write the application or administer its infrastructure to make those decisions. Service names and technical concepts can provide context, but the goal is not to memorize configuration screens. The exam guide prioritizes strategic decisions over implementation. That boundary helps you study efficiently while still becoming a more useful partner to people who build and operate AI systems.

- Understand enough technology to make informed decisions
- Connect capabilities to value, risk and adoption
- Implementation and administration are outside this course focus

### Slide: How Is This Different from AI Practitioner?
<!-- id: m1-ai-practitioner -->
<!-- type: content-image -->
<!-- visual: image -->

AI Practitioner and AI Business Strategist answer different learning needs. Practitioner establishes foundational AI, machine learning and generative AI understanding in an AWS context. This course concentrates on the business decisions surrounding adoption. Imagine two questions about the same proposed assistant. One asks what the technology can do and which broad capabilities are relevant. The other asks whether this organization should fund it, what evidence a pilot must collect and how the work should change if it succeeds. Both conversations matter, and the right starting point depends on your role and existing knowledge. Treat the credentials as ways to demonstrate different capabilities, rather than assuming that one title makes someone better at every aspect of AI.

- AI Practitioner develops foundational AI understanding
- Business Strategist emphasizes business decisions
- Choose according to your role and learning needs

### Slide: A Decision You Will Learn to Make
<!-- id: m1-first-business-decision -->
<!-- type: content-image -->
<!-- visual: svg -->
<!-- figure: slide-m1-first-business-decision -->

Imagine a fictional retailer whose support team is struggling with slow customer responses. A vendor demonstrates an assistant that drafts replies in seconds. Would you approve an immediate rollout? Before answering, ask whether slow drafting is actually the main problem. Perhaps agents spend more time finding accurate information, correcting records or waiting for approvals. Compare the assistant with process improvements and simpler alternatives. If AI still looks promising, define the expected benefit, the full cost, the quality checks and the conditions for stopping. A bounded pilot may then be a reasonable recommendation. Notice that you have already begun a strategic assessment without writing code. We will return to decisions like this throughout the course and make the reasoning progressively more rigorous.

- Start with the business problem
- Compare AI with credible alternatives
- Set evidence and safeguards before scaling

<!-- type: content-image -->

The official outline divides scored content into four areas: AI fundamentals and literacy, twenty-four percent; strategy and business value, twenty-eight percent; governance and responsible AI leadership, twenty-four percent; and readiness, leadership and transformation, twenty-four percent. Think of them as connected lenses on the same decision. You need enough literacy to understand the proposal, an economic argument for pursuing it, controls that address the risks, and an organization capable of adopting it. A strong answer usually respects more than one of those lenses. Our course structure follows the published skills, while examples will connect them so the material does not feel like four unrelated lists. The percentages guide study emphasis; they are not a reason to neglect any one area.

<!-- id: m1-course-outcomes -->
<!-- type: content-image -->
<!-- visual: svg -->
<!-- figure: slide-m1-course-outcomes -->

By the end of the learning path, aim to do more than recognize definitions. Practice producing a short use-case recommendation, a business case with visible assumptions, a risk and accountability plan, and a roadmap for adoption. These do not have to be long documents. A one-page recommendation can be powerful if it makes the problem, alternatives, evidence and decision clear. We will work through examples and ask you to explain why one choice fits the facts better than another. You will also revisit assumptions when the situation changes. That habit makes the learning useful beyond an exam question: it gives you a way to discuss AI proposals with finance, operations, legal and technical colleagues who may each see a different part of the problem.

- Prioritize opportunities and justify a recommendation
- Make assumptions and responsibilities visible
- Plan adoption with measurable decision gates

### Slide: Understand the Beta Exam Before You Book
<!-- id: m1-beta-exam -->
<!-- type: content-image -->
<!-- visual: image -->

The current AWS page describes a beta exam: 170 minutes, 85 multiple-choice or multiple-response questions, and a USD 50 beta price. It lists USD 100 as the standard price, with English and Japanese offered for the beta. A test center and online proctoring are listed as options. The general exam guide separately lists 130 minutes, so do not mix standard-guide details with the beta offer. AWS uses beta testing to evaluate questions for an exam. For planning, use the live certification page and the version shown when you book. The practical lesson is simple: know which exam version you are preparing for, and set aside time for both study and a realistic practice session.

- Beta listing: 170 minutes and 85 questions
- USD 50 beta; USD 100 stated standard price
- Check the live exam version when booking

There is also a reason to look at your study calendar now. AWS currently offers an additional Early Adopter digital badge to people who earn this certification by 15 February 2027. Allow space to learn, practice and address weak areas. ### Slide: Your First Step: Choose a Learning Goal
<!-- id: m1-your-goal -->
<!-- type: content -->

Before moving on, write one sentence beginning: I want to use this course to help me decide. Finish it with a situation that matters to you. Perhaps you want to assess an assistant proposal, lead a responsible pilot, advise a customer or understand where AI fits in a business process. Then note one question you cannot yet answer confidently. You do not need to solve it today. We will return to it as your understanding develops. If the Early Adopter opportunity interests you, add a provisional study milestone, while leaving room to assess readiness honestly. You now have a reason to learn, a view of the path and a question to bring into the next module on AI fundamentals.

- Write one decision you want to make better
- Identify one current knowledge gap
- Bring that goal into the foundations module
